# Fantastic diseases

Generated outbreaks exercise these diseases as ordinary infection episodes.
Environmental reservoirs and a threat vector can produce their clean
traditional profiles, while investigation still requires evidence, chronology,
source identification, and remediation. See
[Outbreak investigations](../strategic/outbreaks.md).

Fantastic diseases use the ordinary strategic health simulation. Their
organisms and toxins are invented, but their transmission, time courses,
functional losses, phenotype variation, and treatment response are physical.
The Humour chart is a lossy historical projection, never a causal model.

## Authoritative mechanics

| Disease | Learned elemental association | Invented biology and route | Coordinated course | Grounded response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mahrdruck | Sylph; air; sanguine | Small nocturnal sylphs roost in shared straw. Their castings contain a symbiont-derived vasoactive agent; the route is infested bedding, not action at a distance. | Perfusion and superficial tissue changes create a predominantly sanguine projection while transient motor inhibition produces the remembered pressure and paralysis. | Replace or isolate bedding through ordinary exposure prevention. Generic topical preparations can limit tissue injury but do not remove the roost. |
| Shroud fever | Nymph; water; phlegmatic | Water nymphs carry an aquatic mould through groundwater and waterlogged graves. Drying shrouds aerosolizes it; patients do not infect one another. | Oxygenation loss precedes neurologic and focal inflammatory effects, producing a predominantly phlegmatic projection with air hunger, cough, and dark oral filaments. | Avoid or contain wet grave material and ventilate drying rooms. Existing neurologic and anti-inflammatory preparations act through their ordinary profiles. |
| Bilwisschuss | Salamander; fire; choleric | Tiny heat-loving salamanders open symbiotic rye galls, releasing toxin-bearing hooked awns. It is an environmental exposure, not a magical projectile. | Temperature loss is followed by inflammation, producing a predominantly choleric projection with fever, focal contracture, and eye irritation. | Leave contaminated grain and clothing, wash exposed material, and use generic fever and inflammation preparations. |
| Kobeldunst | Pygmy; earth; melancholic | Subterranean pygmies cultivate a chemolithotrophic ore film whose metabolism releases organometallic vapour in confined mine air. Contact with the pygmies is not contagious. | Neurologic loss precedes nutrition and tissue effects, producing a predominantly melancholic projection with tremor, fatigue, and confusion. | Fresh air and ventilation stop further exposure. Generic neurologic or tissue preparations retain their ordinary benefits and adverse effects. |

None has a disease-keyed cure, supernatural meter, or class gate. Herbalism
crafts preparations; Physiology recognizes patterns, administers them, and
monitors response. Environmental control uses the same route-prevention
affordances as ordinary disease.

## Diagnostic contract

The four courses deliberately coordinate existing private meters more strongly
than ordinary diseases and cover all four traditional correspondences:
air/sanguine, water/phlegmatic, fire/choleric, and earth/melancholic. The
correspondence is real in these four invented pathologies because their
physical meter bundles happen to project that way. Humours remain neither
substances nor causes and do not become reliable for ordinary disease.

A high-Physiology observer who has authorized bedside history can compare the
*visible sequence* of quantized regional Humour readings against public
definitions. The scorer receives only that sequence, including the actual
timestamps and spacing of authorized observations. It never receives the
patient's infection identity, raw meters, or a reconstructed sample for an
unobserved time. A gap resets the sequence, and a self chart has only its
current snapshot. Before the authored minimum observation window, the ordinary
single-reading differential remains ambiguous. Incidental symptoms and
phenotype variation still create overlap.

Future outbreak, corpse, threat, and environment generators may use the open,
weighted physical evidence IDs `night_sylph_castings`, `nymph_grave_mould`,
`salamander_rye_galls`, and `pygmy_ore_biofilm`. These are evidence, not hidden
diagnosis lookups, and site constraints must remain explicit.

## Historical belief and adaptation notes

These notes describe inspiration, not authoritative biology.

### Paracelsian elemental frame

Paracelsus's *Liber de nymphis, sylphis, pygmaeis et salamandris* associates
nymphs with water, sylphs with air, pygmies with earth, and salamanders with
fire. Its exact composition date is uncertain. Paracelsus died in 1541;
[Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz93903.html?language=en)
records a first abridged posthumous edition in 1566, while the
[New York Public Library catalogue](https://test.nypl.org/research/research-catalog/bib/pb9916825293506421)
describes the complete text used in Huser's Basel edition around 1591. Its use
as an organized, known diagnostic frame in the game's 1544 setting is
therefore a deliberate adaptation: rare manuscript or oral Paracelsian ideas
circulate earlier than the surviving publication record establishes. The
creatures are physical organisms in the simulation, while “elemental” is a
learned classification.

### Mahrdruck / Alpdrücken

- **Attested term:** the *Frühneuhochdeutsches Wörterbuch* entry for
  [Mar](https://www.fwb-online.de/go/mar.s.0m_1709314623) records the nightmare
  being/name and cites Luther material from 1527–1530.
- **Place limitation:** the cited witnesses belong to Luther material and its
  Wittenberg milieu, but the dictionary entry does not establish an exact place
  for every witness. Later northern records from Oldenburg, Saterland, East
  Friesland, and Baltrum are collection evidence from later centuries, not
  proof of a local 1544 Mahr narrative.
- **Adaptation classification:** canonizing the name for the northern-Germany
  setting is a deliberate regional displacement of a period German term from
  an incompletely located source tradition, not a claim of local attestation.
- **Later variants:** pressure, paralysis, and northern European nightmare
  narratives appear in later collections, including the texts gathered in
  [The Mare in Scandinavian Belief](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/nightmare.html#mart).
  Those details are inspiration, not evidence that every motif was current in
  northern Germany in 1544.
- **Deliberate invention:** identifying the nocturnal source as a small sylph,
  its castings and symbiont-derived vasoactive agent, and the ordered
  sanguine-dominant course.

### Nachzehrer

- **Attested context:** Thomas Schürmann, “Der Nachzehrerglauben in
  Mitteleuropa,” *Ethnographisch-Archäologische Zeitschrift* 50 (2009),
  [article PDF](https://www.eaz-journal.org/index.php/eaz/article/download/851/907/2938),
  discusses corpse-chewing/epidemic reports around Silesia and Breslau in
  1516, Luther correspondence around 1540, and Freiberg around 1550.
- **Regional displacement:** this central/eastern German material reaches the
  northern-Germany game setting through travel; it is not presented as a
  universal local belief.
- **Deliberate invention:** water nymphs carrying an aquatic grave mould, its
  spores, delayed reservoir, oral filaments, and the possibility that opening
  a grave worsens exposure. Beheading is an in-world theory, not a causal
  mechanic.

### Bilwisschuss / Belewittenschnitt

- **Attested term:** the *Frühneuhochdeutsches Wörterbuch*
  [Bilwis entry](https://fwb-online.de/lemma/bilwis.s.1f) includes witnesses
  dated 1465, 1484, and before 1486.
- **Place limitation:** those witness dates alone do not establish a northern
  German place of attestation. Middle Low German and Lower Saxon forms such as
  *Belewitte* are later naming evidence; they do not make the staged disease a
  documented northern belief in 1544.
- **Adaptation classification:** the canonical northern use is a deliberate
  regional displacement from incompletely located medieval German witnesses;
  its local crop context is invented rather than presented as attested fact.
- **Inference:** combining those terms into one crop-demon explanation is
  cross-period interpretation, not one uniform 1544 doctrine.
- **Deliberate invention:** heat-loving field salamanders, their symbiotic rye
  galls and toxin-bearing awns, and the staged choleric-dominant course.
- **Rejected candidate:** the concrete Roggenmuhme material considered during
  research is too late for this use: a Saalfeld story is dated 1662 and a Mark
  Brandenburg publication 1816. It is not a canonical disease here.

### Kobeldunst / Bergmännlein

- **Attested context:** Georgius Agricola describes dangerous mine vapours and
  miners' accounts of mine spirits in *De re metallica* (1556), especially
  [Book VI](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/38015/38015-h/38015-h).
  His *De animantibus subterraneis* (1549) is catalogued by the
  [Folger Shakespeare Library](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/367251).
- **Regional caution:** Saxon/Bohemian mining evidence is near-period context.
  Applying it to a Harz mine is plausible displacement; later developed Harz
  legend is not back-projected as 1544 testimony.
- **Deliberate invention:** subterranean pygmies cultivating the ore biofilm,
  its organometallic vapour, and the ordered melancholic-dominant course.
